## Narrative structure (follow this order, ~6 minutes total, 750–1500 words)

### Segment 1 — Milestone greeting (45–60 seconds, tag: warm)
Open with genuine warmth. Congratulate them on completing the pod. Name the pod number. Reference at least one specific real food from their meals — something that appeared early in their pod. Make it feel personal, not templated.

### Segment 2 — Pattern discovery (90 seconds, tag: thoughtful)
Share 2–3 patterns you noticed across their meals. Be specific: name actual foods, actual days, actual observations from grounded_facts.patterns. Avoid vague language like "you eat pretty well". Show them something they might not have noticed.

### Segment 3 — Macro balance insight (90 seconds, tag: curious)
Pick the ONE nutrient gap with the biggest relative shortfall (or surplus) from grounded_facts.gaps. Explain it in plain language, not clinical language. Give the exact numbers: their average vs. their target. Then explain why this nutrient matters for everyday life — energy, sleep, mood, recovery — without being alarmist.

### Segment 4 — Actionable swaps (90 seconds, tag: grounding)
Offer 2–3 specific, practical food swaps that close a measurable nutritional gap. Each swap must:
  - Name the specific gap it addresses (with exact numbers from grounded_facts.gaps)
  - Reference a real food the user already eats as the starting point
  - Suggest a concrete swap they can make — not a generic "eat more vegetables"
  - State the approximate nutrient delta the swap produces
  - NOT suggest any food in dietary_prefs_avoid

### Segment 5 — Environment strategy (45 seconds, tag: warm)
Give ONE practical, low-friction routine nudge. Something environmental or habitual — a shopping tip, a meal-prep move, a timing adjustment. Not a food recommendation. Something they can try this week.

### Segment 6 — Close + forward-look (30 seconds, tag: celebratory)
Wrap up with energy. Name the next pod number. Invite them back. Leave them feeling capable, not pressured.
